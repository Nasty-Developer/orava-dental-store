data and its existing research. This document records what actually exists, what
was verified, and what is genuinely missing. Nothing here is a plan.

The gap between 339 photographs on file and 0 served is the single most important

### Data fields that do not exist on the original 344 rows

`sourceUrl`, `verifiedOn`, `imagePermission`, `model`, `packSize`: zero occurrences
across the catalogue. The type definition supports `source` and `verification`, but
the original 344 rows predate those fields and carry neither. `docs/EXPANSION-METHOD.md`
already states this plainly: the first 344 rows were harvested from a retailer
listing before the no-duplication rule existed, and are a weaker provenance class.

## 5. Bugs and incomplete functionality found

1. **Stale consumer SEO copy on `/shop`.** The meta description still reads
   "enamel-safe whitening, sonic brushes, alcohol-free rinses, clinical gels and
   orthodontic kits" — copy from an earlier consumer oral-care positioning. It is
   wrong for a clinical supplies store and it is what search engines show.
2. **339 photographs on file, none served.** 8.7 MB of images ship in the
   repository while every card shows a placeholder. Not a bug in itself (the gate
   is deliberate), but the repository carries the weight without the benefit.
3. **No provenance on the original 344 rows.** No source URL, no verification date,
   no image permission field. Stage 3 of the brief is therefore a real repair job,
   not a formality.
4. **The catalogue is bundled for the client.** `shop-client.tsx` imports the whole
   `products` array, so every product record ships to the browser. At 344 rows the
   shop chunk is 18 KB. At 3,000 rows this becomes a multi-megabyte payload and the
   shop page will visibly slow down. This is the one architectural limit that must
   be fixed before the catalogue grows.
5. **No database is provisioned.** `db/schema.sql` describes a Supabase schema for
   products, orders and enquiries with row-level security. The storefront does not
   read from it and no instance exists.
6. **Five products still have no photograph at all**, not merely a withheld one.
7. **Repository and project names disagree** with the brand (`orava` vs `somil`).

## 6. What is genuinely good and must not be rebuilt

- Honest product metadata: no invented ratings, no review counts, no `compareAt`
  fake discounts, no stock quantities, no delivery promises. JSON-LD emits an
  `Offer` only when a price is actually known.
- Sitemap, canonical URLs, robots, breadcrumb and product structured data.
- The rights gate and the expansion gate, both enforced in code.
- Per-product pages, related products by genuine category, brand and category
  filters, sorting, cart, and the WhatsApp order composer.
- A clean build: 383 static pages, no errors.

## 7. The honest gap to the brief

The brief asks for 3,000+ verified products and exact authorised photography. The
constraints in the brief are what make that slow, and they are the right
constraints:

- Every row needs a manufacturer or authorised-distributor page that was actually
  opened. Retailer listings are discovery only. `docs/EXPANSION-METHOD.md` already
  measured this as a multi-session job at tranches of 150–200 rows.
- No photograph can go live without a recorded permission state. 339 are withheld
  today, 27 Waldent are staged behind one written yes, and the remaining brands
  publish no machine-readable catalogue at all — their photographs have to come
  from a media kit reply or from Tanvi's own camera.
- Two of the brief's own gates require a human decision: approving newly researched
  products for publication, and approving the exact recipient, subject and body of
  every permission email.

So the catalogue grows only as fast as verification and permission allow. This
audit does not claim otherwise, and no count in this document is estimated.
